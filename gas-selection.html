Assist Gas Selection

The assist gas does two jobs: it blows molten metal out of the kerf, and in the case of oxygen it adds heat through an exothermic reaction. Choosing the gas is usually a choice between edge quality, speed and cost.

GasMaterialsEdge resultCost per cutting hour
Oxygen (O₂)Mild steel, 1–25 mmRough, oxidized edge; weldable after cleaningLow (~$4)
Nitrogen (N₂)Stainless, aluminum, 0.5–12 mmBright, oxide-free edge; ready for passivationHigh (~$18)
Compressed airMild steel, aluminum (non-critical)Rougher than N₂, some oxidationLow (compressor)

Rules of thumb

  • Cutting mild steel for welding: oxygen gives the speed; clean the edge before welding.
  • Cutting stainless that goes to passivation or a food-grade finish: nitrogen, no question.
  • Cutting aluminum: nitrogen, with a clean oxide-free surface before piercing.
  • Cutting copper or brass: nitrogen at high pressure; expect slow speeds regardless of gas.

Gas cost dominates the hourly rate when nitrogen is on — see the cost breakdown in the cost per hour guide.
