Laser Cutting Kerf: What Determines Its Width

Kerf width affects nesting, tolerance and how small a hole you can cut reliably.

Kerf is the width of material the beam removes. On fiber lasers it typically runs 0.2-0.5 mm, depending on power, lens and material.

What changes kerf

  • Beam spot size (lens and focus).
  • Power density at the workpiece.
  • Material thickness and gas pressure.

Why it matters

Kerf decides the smallest hole you can cut without tapering, the offset you need in nesting, and the fit of parts that interlock. A 0.1 mm kerf error across a large nest can mean a part does not fit.
