Why Stainless Cuts Better with Nitrogen

The assist gas chemistry decides whether a stainless edge needs post-processing.

Stainless steel cut with oxygen gets an oxidized edge. Cut with nitrogen, the edge stays bright and weldable without pickling.

What nitrogen does

Nitrogen is inert in the cut: it blows molten metal out without reacting. Oxygen, by contrast, adds exothermic heat and leaves oxide.

Practical trade-offs

  • Nitrogen is consumed at high flow and costs more per hour than oxygen.
  • Oxygen cuts faster on thicker stainless but leaves a rough edge that needs grinding.
  • For parts going straight to welding or passivation, nitrogen wins.

For material-specific behavior see the 304 vs 316 stainless guide. Hypertherm publishes assist-gas guidance worth reading.
