Heat-Affected Zone: What It Does to the Part

A narrow HAZ is one of laser cutting's main advantages. Here is what it changes in the metal and when it matters.

The heat-affected zone (HAZ) is the bond of metal beside the cut whose microstructure changed from the heat. Fiber laser cutting produces a narrow HAZ, which is why it is preferred for hardened or stress-sensitive parts.

What changes

Hardened steels can soften in the HAZ; some alloys become more corrosion-sensitive. For most mild steel fabrication the HAZ is irrelevant to the function.

When it matters

  • Heat-treated components where hardness must survive.
  • Parts that will be fatigue-loaded.
  • Materials where corrosion resistance depends on the surface layer.

Where heat cannot enter at all, waterjet is the answer. For the process trade-off see the process comparison.
