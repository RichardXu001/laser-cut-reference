ISO 9013 Cut Quality Classes

ISO 9013 defines thermal cutting quality classes by two measurable properties: perpendicularity (or perpendicularity tolerance) and mean roughness depth. A drawing that says "laser cut" without a class has defined nothing — a shop may legally deliver the worst acceptable edge.

What the standard measures

The standard expresses quality as ranges such as 1–2, 2–4, 3–5, where the numbers are perpendicularity tolerance ranges in millimetres over the material thickness. Lower numbers mean a straighter edge. Surface roughness is quoted separately as Ra or Rz values.

Typical classPerpendicularity range (mm)Edge lookTypical use
1–20.1–0.2Straight, fine striationsVisible or weld-critical edges
2–40.2–0.4Light taper, visible at close inspectionGeneral fabrication
3–50.3–0.5Noticeable taper, rough top edgeNon-visible structural parts

Exact ranges depend on the class number and the material thickness; the table above shows the common ranges quoted by machine builders. Always confirm against the current ISO 9013 text before writing a specification.

How to specify a class

Put the class on the drawing next to the edge symbol — for example, "ISO 9013, class 2–4". If the part goes to welding, specify the edge condition that the welding procedure needs, because a class number alone does not define weldability.
